Passel sends links and notes between your own devices. It is run by [OPERATOR LEGAL NAME], [POSTAL ADDRESS] ("we"). Questions about this policy go to [CONTACT EMAIL].

In short

  • We keep what you send (links and notes) so your other devices can show it, until you delete it.
  • From your Google account we keep only its unique id. We don't store your email address or your name.
  • To show a preview of a link, our server visits the linked page once.
  • What you send is not end-to-end encrypted: our server can read it, as it must to deliver it.
  • We don't sell your data, show ads, or put third-party analytics or trackers in our apps or on this site.

What we collect and why

Your account

You sign in with Google. Each request your apps make carries a Google ID token, which our server checks against Google's published keys. From the token we keep one thing: your Google account's unique id, stored with each item you send so that only you can see it. The token also holds your email address and name; your apps show the email address on screen, but our server doesn't store either. We never see your Google password, and signing in gives us no access to anything else in your Google account.

What you send

For a link, we store its address and the name it was sent with, if any; for a note, its text and title, if any. With each item we store when it was sent and an id of our own. Sending files is not available yet; this policy will say what we keep for them before it is. Items are stored in our database, which we host with [HOSTING PROVIDER] in [REGION].

Link previews

When you send a link to a web page, our server fetches that page once, shortly afterwards, to read the title, description and image address the page gives for itself. It only fetches public http and https addresses, never addresses on private networks, follows at most three redirects, reads at most 512 KB, and gives up after three attempts. The site sees a request from our server, which identifies itself as Passel/1.0 (+https://passel.link); nothing about you is sent with it. We store the title, description, image address and the time of the fetch with your link, and delete them with it.

Our server never downloads the preview image. When an app shows a preview, your device loads the image straight from the site that hosts it, as with any image on a web page, so that site can see your device's IP address. Passel's web app sends no referrer with it. Today the web app shows previews; the Chrome extension and the Android app will later.

Live updates

While one of your apps is open and visible, it keeps a connection to our server open, so that what you send from one device appears on the others straight away. Nothing extra is stored for it.

Limits on use

To keep Passel usable for everyone, our server counts the requests each account makes (or each IP address, for requests that aren't signed in) over the last minute, in memory only, and checks how many items and how much storage an account uses: up to 5,000 items and 50 MB today. The counts aren't stored.

Technical logs

Our server records logs, traces and metrics to keep the service running and to fix it: for example which part of the service was called, when, how long it took and what it answered, errors with our own ids for the items involved, and the addresses of pages fetched for previews. They do not contain the text of your notes. They are kept by [MONITORING PROVIDER] for [LOG RETENTION PERIOD].

What stays on your devices

  • Web app: your Google sign-in token, in the tab's session storage until you close the tab or sign out, and a note in the browser's local storage that you signed in with Google, so that coming back can offer one-tap sign-in; signing out removes it. Your items are kept in the open tab's memory only. The sign-in page loads Google's sign-in script from Google, which Google's privacy policy covers.
  • Chrome extension: your sign-in token, in the browser's session storage, which is cleared when the browser closes; your email address, so it can sign you back in; and your 50 newest items, so they show at once. Signing out removes the address and the items.
  • Android app: the id of the account last signed in and your 50 newest items, in the app's own storage; Google's sign-in service on the phone holds the token. Signing out removes the items.

How long we keep it

Items are kept until you delete them; nothing expires on its own today. Deleting an item erases it from our database at once, together with its preview, and your other devices remove it as soon as they hear about it. Backups of the database kept by our hosting provider may hold a deleted item for up to [BACKUP RETENTION PERIOD].

The apps can't yet delete your whole account in one step. Until they can, delete your items, or write to [CONTACT EMAIL] and we will delete everything stored under your account.
